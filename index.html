The Problem

You hold your business to a standard. Hold yourself to it, too.

You make decisions under pressure and carry responsibility for other people’s results. Your own health keeps getting whatever is left.

You know where you’ve let yourself slip.

  • You look at a photo from a day you should remember fondly. But instead of seeing the moment, all you can focus on is how much weight you’ve gained.
  • Your kids ask you to get in the water with them. You want to. But the thought of taking your shirt off is enough to keep you on the sand.
  • Your business gets the sharpest, most driven version of you. By the time you walk through the front door, your partner and kids get the exhausted, distracted version with nothing left to give.
  • You don’t feel the same confidence undressing in front of your partner anymore. Even in the moments when you should feel closest, you’re thinking about how you look.
  • You keep promising yourself you’ll get back in shape when business settles down. But there’s always another deal, another deadline, another problem. And another year goes by with nothing changing.

You have already given this enough time to fix itself. The next step is a strategy you can execute through the life you actually have.

The man should be impressive without the business attached to him.

The result has to work in your life.

Your relationship, family and business are part of the strategy from the start. We plan around long days, travel, restaurants and time with the people who matter.

The goal is a body you are proud of and the physical capacity to be involved in your life. The change needs to show up when you leave the gym, too.

You already have enough to manage.

I review what is working, identify what needs to change and adapt the strategy when your circumstances change. You have a clear plan and someone personally responsible for keeping it relevant.

Your responsibility is execution. Mine is making sure you are executing the right strategy.
